Some Companies Just Dropped a Nitaqat Tier — Without Changing Their Workforce

NIKKI ADVISORY June 27, 2026 2 min read

A company's Saudi-to-expatriate ratio can stay exactly the same and its Nitaqat classification can still fall.

That sounds like a contradiction. It isn't.

In April 2026, MHRSD launched a new three-year Nitaqat cycle. The formula didn't change. The "c-values" underneath it — the figures that convert a workforce ratio into a tier — were raised across most sectors. A business that did nothing differently can find itself a full band lower than it was in March.

The Yellow tier disappeared in the same update. It used to function as a buffer — a warning zone before Red. Companies that sat there have moved directly into Red, with blocked work permit renewals and restricted government services. One consequence many employers don't expect: expatriate staff at Red-tier companies can transfer their own sponsorship without the employer's consent.

For many businesses, the commercial impact is greater than the compliance impact. Delayed hiring, restricted government services, and unplanned talent mobility can affect project delivery, client commitments, and expansion timelines just as directly as any contract risk.

There is a second, quieter change that may matter more.

Since 15 April 2026, a Saudi employee only counts toward your Saudization percentage if their contract is digitally documented and counter-signed on Qiwa. GOSI registration alone is no longer sufficient. A company can have genuinely employed Saudi staff on payroll and still register as non-compliant because the paperwork sits in the wrong system.

Quotas are also no longer purely company-wide.

Profession-specific requirements now apply across 269 roles. A business can be Green at the headline level and still be in violation inside a single department — because that one team doesn't meet its own quota.

Before your next hiring decision, three questions are worth asking:

  1. Has our Nitaqat band changed under the new c-values?
  2. Are all Saudi employment contracts digitally documented in Qiwa?
  3. Are profession-specific quotas creating exposure we haven't mapped?

For businesses already operating in the Kingdom, the useful action this week is an audit, not a hiring decision — checking your current band against the new c-values and confirming every Saudi contract is properly documented.

For investors entering Saudi Arabia now, Saudization can no longer sit downstream of the formation process. It has to be designed into the structure from the first hire.

The businesses most affected by Nitaqat are rarely the ones hiring too few Saudis. They are often the ones managing compliance too late.
